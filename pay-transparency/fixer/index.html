How it works

2We check the laws for your location

FAQ

Questions, answered

Do you make up a pay range for me?

No, never. The pay in your fixed ad comes only from your ad or from the numbers you type. If a range is missing, the ad comes back with [ADD LOWEST PAY] and a question for you. Laws ask for the range you in good faith expect to pay, and only you know that.

Is my job ad uploaded anywhere?

No. The fixer runs as JavaScript inside your browser tab. Nothing you paste is sent to Steadfeed or anyone else, and it is gone when you close the page. It uses no AI service. Our only analytics is cookie-free Cloudflare Web Analytics, which counts page visits and never sees what you type.

Which laws does it follow?

The US state and city job-posting pay laws in force on the day you use it: California, Colorado, Connecticut, D.C., Hawaii, Illinois, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New Jersey, New York, Vermont, Virginia and Washington, plus New York City, Westchester County, Albany County, Ithaca, Jersey City and Cleveland. Columbus, Ohio switches on Jan 1, 2027 and Delaware on Sep 26, 2027. We re-read each official source on Nov 15, 2026.

Why does it ask about benefits and deadlines?

Several laws ask for more than pay. Colorado, Connecticut, Illinois, Maryland, Minnesota, New Jersey, Washington and Jersey City ask for a general description of benefits. Colorado also asks for the application deadline and how to apply. The fixer only asks what the laws for your location need.

What about remote jobs?

A remote job open to US applicants can be reached by several state laws, so the fixer applies all of them unless you tell it where you hire. That usually means adding a benefits line, an application deadline and how to apply.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is a drafting aid that helps you meet the posting rules we track, as we read them on the date shown. It does not certify anything. Employer-size counts, exceptions and remote-work rules vary, so check the official source linked next to each change, or ask an employment lawyer.

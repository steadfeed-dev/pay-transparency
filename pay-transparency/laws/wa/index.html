Washington pay transparency law for job postings

What a Washington job posting needs to show, who the rule covers, and the potential issues we see most often. Plain English, with the official source linked.

Checked November 17, 2026Official source linked

In effect sinceJanuary 1, 2023
Who’s covered15 or more employees
What a posting showsThe wage scale or salary range (or the fixed wage if there is no range), and a general description of all benefits and other compensation.

Official source: app.leg.wa.gov

A 5-business-day correction window (through July 27, 2027)

For postings made from July 27, 2025 through July 27, 2027, anyone can give an employer written notice that a posting is missing required information. If the employer corrects the posting within five business days of that notice, and asks any third-party job board carrying it to correct it too, no penalties or damages can be awarded for that violation. The window applies to each posting for as long as it is up.

Fixed pay is allowed

Since July 27, 2025, a posting may show a fixed wage instead of a range when only one amount is offered.

What’s at stake

Job applicants can bring a claim, and statutory damages run from $100 to $5,000 per violation (the department or court weighs factors such as employer size and whether a violation was willful). Daily checks are a simple way to catch a missing range or benefits line well inside the 5-day window.

Common potential issues

  • “DOE”, “competitive” or “negotiable” in place of real numbers.
  • Open-ended figures such as “up to $X” or “$80k+”.
  • No general description of benefits (a one-line summary is enough).
  • The same job reposted on a job board or by a recruiter without the range.
  • Remote roles open to applicants in this state that show no pay at all.

How to write an Etsy title that gets found

Etsy changed its advice. Long, keyword-stuffed titles are out. Short, clear titles that say what you made are in. Here’s how to write one, with examples and a checklist you can use today.

7-minute readUpdated November 2026Based on Etsy’s own Seller Handbook

Why the old “cram every keyword in” advice stopped working

For years, sellers filled all 140 characters with every phrase a shopper might type, like gift for her, gift for mom, birthday gift, Christmas gift. Etsy now says plainly that its search takes a holistic view of your listing. It looks at your title, tags, attributes, description, first photo and even reviews. Your title still matters for search and for clicks, but it no longer has to carry everything.

The bigger reason is people. In search results, shoppers see only the first few words of your title, especially on a phone. If those words are “Gift for Her, Mom Gift, Birthday…”, nobody can tell what you made. A clear title helps a shopper decide in a second whether to tap.

A simple formula

What it ismug, crossword poster, earrings+ 2–3 key traitsmaterial, color, size or format+ What makes it yourspersonalized, hand-thrown, made from your photo

Say the item once, early. Then add the facts a shopper uses to rule an item in or out, such as color, material, size, format, or what they get. Stop there. Etsy suggests fewer than 15 words. If the title is longer, something in it probably belongs somewhere else.

Mention an occasion or a recipient only when it is what the item is. A birthday candle or a Halloween costume passes that test. Gift for him does not.

Nothing useful was lost. Gift for her, bridesmaid gift and anniversary gift moved into the tags. Beautiful and perfect moved into the description, where you have room to explain why.

Let your 13 tags do the heavy lifting

Tags are where the phrases you cut from the title go. Etsy’s Help Center gives the rules:

  • Use up to 13 tags, each up to 20 characters. A tag can be a short phrase like silver earrings.
  • Tags are where buyer-minded phrases like gift for her belong. They also help when no attribute fits.
  • Use a diverse mix of phrases. Thirteen versions of the same two words waste slots.
  • Use letters, numbers and spaces. An apostrophe or hyphen can go inside a tag but can’t start one.

A good mix covers what it is (crossword poster), who it’s for (gift for wife), the occasion (paper anniversary) and the style (love story print).

Back it up with your first sentence and first photo

Your title, first description sentence and first photo work as a team. Open the description by naming the item plainly, for example: “This is a personalized crossword poster made from your own clues, delivered as print-ready PDFs.” Save the story for the next paragraph.

Etsy’s listing guide suggests keeping the first photo free of text overlays and collages. Use the other photo slots, up to 20 per listing, for close-ups, scale and how it’s used. You can also add up to two videos of 5 to 15 seconds.

Before you edit: a safe four-step routine

  1. Download a CSV of your listings first. Etsy recommends this so you keep your original keywords to reuse in tags and descriptions.
  2. Start with two or three listings. Use the ones with the most views, so you can see what changes.
  3. Note your numbers. Write down visits, views and favorites from Shop Manager Stats so you have a “before”.
  4. Give it time, then compare. Editing a title doesn’t change the listing’s URL, so your links and pins keep working.

Etsy also offers an optional title-suggestion tool for eligible English listings. Find it on your search visibility page in Shop Manager or in the Etsy Seller app. Treat its suggestions as a starting point. You decide what gets published.

The 60-second checklist

  • The first two or three words say what the item is.
  • Key traits like material, color, size or format come next.
  • It’s under 15 words, and no word repeats.
  • There are no gift lists, no “perfect” or “beautiful”, and no sale or shipping talk.
  • It names an occasion only if the occasion is the item.
  • All 13 tags are used, each a different phrase of 20 characters or fewer.
  • The first description sentence names the item, too.
